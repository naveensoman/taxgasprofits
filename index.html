Why I started this

I grew up in the Gulf. Oil and gas money was everywhere: in the schools, the hospitals and the roads. Also in a lot of Italian supercars. I'm not asking for those.

When I became Australian, I assumed a country this rich in gas and minerals would have the same to show for it. Then I looked at the numbers.

We ship about as much gas as Qatar. Our gas profits tax raised about $51 each.

That $51 was 2025-26.2,3,29 The beer tax raised more.2 And when people get angry about housing, politicians point at migrants like me instead of the tax settings they wrote. I want less migration too, matched to the homes we actually build. It's just not the whole fix.

I'm not asking for Qatar. Qatar keeps its gas money for a few hundred thousand citizens, a small minority of the people who live there. There are 27.9 million of us.3,30 I'm asking for Norway, a democracy that taxes petroleum profits at 78% and has saved about $600,000 for every person in the country.23,31–34 Our government's fund holds $10,400 for each of us.24

That gap isn't bad luck. Both major parties chose it, again and again, and then defended it. I chose this country. I want it to get a fair price for what it sells.

In 2025 we shipped about as much liquefied natural gas (LNG) as Qatar.29 Qatar's gas belongs mostly to its state company.35 Ours is run by companies like Chevron, Shell, Woodside and Japan's INPEX, whose biggest shareholder is the Japanese government.36

Japan was our biggest customer, buying about a third.29 The Australia Institute, a progressive think tank, estimates that Japan's own tax on the Australian gas it imports raises about $710 million a year. That's more than the $420 million a year our gas profits tax has collected from LNG exporters since mid-2023, and about half of what it collects from everyone.2,13,14 Honestly, good on Japan. At least someone's charging for it.

Why so little? The tax lets a project carry its unused costs forward, grow them each year, and take them off later income. The 2023 Budget pointed to projects with “very large carry-forward deductions that are uplifted for many years”, and said many LNG projects weren't expected to pay much “until the 2030s”. That Budget announced a cap: from seven years into production, LNG projects pay on at least a tenth of their income.8 It isn't for want of profit. Shell told the Senate it made $2.5 billion before tax in 2025, the year it paid $109 million in gas profits tax.12

Buyers: Japan, China and others, mostly on long-term contracts.27 Tax settings: both major parties.

You're not imagining it.

Dear homeowner: this isn't about you. It's about the rules.

If you own the home you live in, a higher price mostly lives on a property website. Sell to move, and the next home has gone up too, plus stamp duty.125 There's nowhere cheaper to go, either: by September 2025 a regional home cost 8.1 times income, against 8.2 in the capitals.4

The gain turns into spending money only if you sell and buy less, or borrow against it. That means downsizers, people moving overseas, investors selling to the next buyer, and heirs. The Productivity Commission says people inherit at about 50 on average, which is a long wait for a deposit.126

The bill goes to whoever buys next: the young, people who came here to build a life, and our own kids. They're bidding against people who already own and bring their gains with them. Existing owners take about 65% of new owner-occupier home loans.4 Home ownership among 30-to-34-year-olds fell from 64% in 1971 to 50% in 2021.127

Then there are the rules. Since 1999, investors have paid tax on only half their capital gains.75 In 2026 Labor passed a law to wind that back from July 2027. It also limited negative gearing to new builds, but let every investor who'd already bought keep it. The Coalition has promised to undo both.76,109 So here's the slogan neither of them will put on a corflute.

You're a millionaire. Valid only with a one-way ticket out.

Our plan

This time, we're setting a reserve.

Charge for the gas. Build the homes. Bank the rest. Some of this the Greens want, and some of it the Coalition wants.109,128 We want all of it, and we answer to neither.

Tax gas profits properly.

Norway's tax rate on petroleum profits is 78%.31 By the Superpower Institute's estimate, Australia takes about 18% of the gas industry's cash flow in tax, against more than 75% elsewhere.12

Our policy: a levy on gas profits above a normal return, on top of company tax, modelled on Norway's. It starts at 50% to make up for lost time, then settles at 40%. At 40%, the Superpower Institute estimates a levy like this would raise about $13 billion a year on average to 2050. Because it taxes only profits above a normal return, it doesn't push up prices.129

Even at 40%, that's nearly five times the beer tax.2,129

House everyone first.

122,494 people were homeless on Census night 2021, the latest count.7 Housing them comes before any tax cut.

Stop making Australians pay a fortune to stay in Australia.

If every home doubles, a family that stays put gets a bigger number, not a bigger house. Their next home doubled too, and so did their kids' first one. In the five years to September 2025, home values rose 53.5% and household incomes 20%.4 Finish the job on negative gearing and the capital gains discount.76 Investors who already own keep today's rules for five more years, then move to the same rules as everyone else. The home you live in stays free of capital gains tax, as it is now.76,130 Build public and community housing, and don't stop. If you've just bought, we're not out to crash your price. We want house prices to stand still while incomes catch up.

Match migration to homes.

Set each year's intake by the number of homes finished the year before. Nobody should arrive in a country with nowhere to live, and nobody already here should be left without a home either. The Coalition now promises a cap like this too. It also promises to bring back the investor tax breaks. We wouldn't.108,109

Bank it, and pay every Australian a dividend.

Put the money in a national wealth fund, as Norway does, and pay a yearly dividend from it, as Alaska has paid its residents since 1982. In 2026 it was US$1,200 a person, including a US$200 energy relief payment.131

Legalise cannabis. Then actually tax it.

Let adults grow and buy it legally. The ACT has allowed home growing since 2020.132 The Parliamentary Budget Office costed the Greens' plan for a national legal market at an $8.7 billion budget gain over the 11 years to 2035-36.128

The first crop we'd charge properly for.

What the gas money pays for.

Homes come first. The gas money pays for more than homes.

Make things here again.

In 1978 about one Australian job in five was in manufacturing.133 Today it's about one in seventeen, and still falling: down 8,700 in the year to February 2026.134 We mine about a third of the world's lithium and ship most of it overseas as crushed rock, mostly to China.135,136 In February 2026 Albemarle idled the last working line at its Kemerton lithium refinery in WA.137

Gas first for Australian factories. Labor's gas reservation passed the House on 29 October 2026. It reserves "up to" 20% of exports, less gas already sold under contracts signed by 22 December 2025, from 1 January 2028.138,139 We'd set a firm floor and start sooner. Then use the gas money to refine our lithium and process our minerals here, through the $15 billion National Reconstruction Fund and processing credits bigger and sooner than today's 10% from 2027-28.140,141 Keep our last two oil refineries open past 2030: the Coalition started paying them to stay open in 2021, and Labor extended it to 2030.142,143 And train our own engineers and tradies, and give them something to build.

Dig it up, ship it out, buy it back. We'd like to try it the other way round.

Real learning first. Screens second.

Our 15-year-olds' reading is 37 points lower than in 2000, and maths is 46 points lower than in 2003. Both fell again from 2022 to 2025. Only 55% now meet the national standard in reading, and 49% in maths.144 It isn't computer skills: our students scored well above the OECD average in PISA's new digital learning test.145 Across the OECD, students distracted by devices in most maths lessons scored 15 points lower, and more Australian students report that distraction than the OECD average.146 A review of 54 studies with over 170,000 readers found people understand informational texts a little better on paper.147

Phones are now banned or restricted in government schools in every state and territory.148 That's a start. From 2027 Victoria will cap screen time in its government primary schools. We'd cap it everywhere.149 Paper and pen for reading, writing and maths basics. A printed textbook for every child in every core subject, as Sweden began funding in 2024.150 And explicit teaching, step by step, as the default.151

Our kids are already good at screens. Let's get them good at reading again.

Then run it straight.

A fair price for the gas needs a government that answers for who it hires, how people are treated at work, and what it signs. And every family needs police who investigate properly.

Give the job to the best person, not the best mate.

In 2022, 64 of the 320 members of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal were connected to the party that appointed them. In 2024, full-time members earned up to $530,630 a year.114 Labor replaced that tribunal in October 2024. By law, its members, apart from judges, must now be found suitable by a panel, through an advertised, merit-based process.116

Boards are another story. A review for the Albanese government found friends of the government had “too often” been put on boards, in appointments that looked like “forms of patronage and nepotism”. It said both major parties do it, and that little would make a fix stick without a law.161 The government's new rules for boards and top public service jobs aren't law.162 Do for every board and top job what Labor did for one tribunal, and go further. Advertise every job, let an independent panel draw up the shortlist, and write it into law.

Your mates can still apply. On the same website as everyone else.

Stop racism at work before anyone has to complain.

Since 2022, federal law has made employers take reasonable steps to prevent sexual harassment. The Racial Discrimination Act has no such duty.119,163,164 In the federal public service, 77% of staff who said they'd faced discrimination of any kind didn't report it. Half of those didn't think anything would be done.165 In 2024-25, when the Human Rights Commission tried to settle a race complaint between the two sides, it worked 42% of the time. That was the worst rate under the four federal discrimination laws.166

Put the duty in the Act and let the Human Rights Commission enforce it, as the Commission itself recommends.119

Under federal law, employers must work to prevent sexual harassment. For racism, they can wait for the complaint form.

Ban outsourcing the government's own job.

The Coalition capped public service numbers from 2015. A Labor-chaired Senate committee found the cap pushed work to labour-hire firms and contractors. Coalition senators disagreed.167,168 Labor scrapped the cap in 2022 and set targets to bring core work back in-house. The Public Service Commission said the first year's target was beaten. The Auditor-General found agencies could meet the targets while spending more on outsiders overall. The targets were dropped in July 2026.167,169

Our law: if it's the government's own job, a public servant does it. Specialists, short bursts of extra work and independent audits are still fine. If a labour-hire worker fills a role for 12 months, it becomes a public service job. And every big contract gets published in full, fine print included, with only real security secrets blacked out.

A big new building needs Parliament's tick. In 2023, more than $3 billion of consulting and services contracts didn't.167

Investigate every call. Protect the right person.

Most victims of domestic violence are women. Since the age of 15, 1 in 6 women and 1 in 18 men have experienced violence from a partner they lived with.152 Police must protect every victim. Queensland's police inquiry found officers too often didn't investigate properly, so some victims, especially Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander women, were wrongly named as the abuser.170 In NSW, police charged a woman on her ex-partner's account, and she spent over two months in custody. In that time he admitted he had lied. The state's police watchdog said officers' training and rules gave too little guidance on telling the main aggressor from the victim.171 Anyone wrongly accused, woman or man, deserves a proper investigation and a fair hearing. That doesn't mean arresting fewer abusers: arrest helps reduce repeat violence.172

States and territories run police and courts. All of them agreed, with the Commonwealth, to the National Plan to End Violence against Women and Children, which aims to stop the wrong person being named as the abuser.158 We'd hold every government to it, at National Cabinet and through the Commonwealth's family violence funding.173 A body independent of police should investigate complaints about police. NSW and Queensland have independent watchdogs, but both send most complaints back to police.170,174 Queensland's 2022 inquiry recommended a civilian-led unit to handle all of them. In 2026 the state was still assessing options.175

If you or someone you know is experiencing violence or is at risk of it: 1800RESPECT 1800 737 732, or text 0458 737 732. If you are a man dealing with relationship problems, stress or family violence: MensLine Australia 1300 78 99 78. If you are worried about your own behaviour: Men's Referral Service 1300 766 491. All three take calls 24/7. If someone is in danger, call 000.160

Set the standard. Publish the results. Help both people understand.

We'd push for a duty on police everywhere to investigate domestic violence, so victims aren't left to protect themselves alone.176–178 Its minimum standard would never delay protection, arrest, charging or bail, or need a victim's statement. Once it's safe, police would give each adult a chance to speak separately, with independent interpreters when needed, and check both people's domestic violence history, not just the latest incident.170,179 They'd record this, and who most needs protection, before a long-term protection order they seek or issue is made, or a charge goes past its first court date. Courts could check the record, without revealing how to find a victim. Any gap would mean a short deadline for police, never lost protection. If either person is a police officer, another district would investigate.171

Let courts correct records naming the wrong person, with both people heard safely, and make police bring their own mistakes to court at once.179 We'd fund the ABS to add protection order cases, and the sex of the person protected, to its yearly count of how domestic violence charges end, by state.180,181 That includes the reasons courts recorded and how many records were corrected, whatever the numbers show.182 A dropped or dismissed case doesn't mean the complaint was false, and each report should say so. Fear of the abuser and worry about children are among the most common reasons victims take back statements to police.172

The person protected and the person named should be offered free advice from different legal services before deciding anything about an order.183,184 It would explain the case plainly, with an independent interpreter when needed: what an order bans, and that breaking one is a crime.180 Anyone at risk would stay protected meanwhile. Neither person would question the other in court themselves. A free lawyer would ask the questions instead, as Victoria mostly requires already.185 We'd fund the advice with new money through the national legal aid agreement, not from victims' services.186

Keep the peace.

Defend Australia. Stay out of wars of choice. Hold every government to the same law.

Defend Australia. Fight no one else's wars.

Both major parties say "deterrence", and so do we. The government's 2026 strategy says it "aims to deter any conflict", but it also gives the ADF the task to "Contribute with our partners to the collective security of the Indo-Pacific".187 We'd defend this continent and its sea and air approaches, and stop there. Today no vote of Parliament is needed to send troops to war: the 2023 inquiry kept that decision with Cabinet, and the government agreed.188,189 Our law: no Australian forces into armed conflict overseas without a vote of both Houses, except to defend against an attack.

Estimates put AUKUS at $268-368 billion; the government prefers to call it about 0.15% of GDP.190 We'd leave it. The core treaty can be ended on one year's notice, and the related deals would also have to be unwound.191 We'd spend instead on what defends Australia: drones, missiles, mines, air defence and submarines built here. Most Australians still back AUKUS (68%), but trust in the US is at a record low of 31%. We're asking them to think again.192

Australian soil, Australian decisions.

The government says there are "no foreign military bases on Australian soil".193 Under the 2014 Force Posture Agreement, US forces still have "unimpeded access to and use of" agreed Australian facilities, without rent, though the US shares the running and building costs.194,195 Defence calls Pine Gap "an intelligence collection facility".196 Its own definition of Australia's "full knowledge and concurrence" says that "does not mean Australia approves every activity or tasking".197

We'd give notice to end these agreements: one year for the Force Posture Agreement, which covers the Darwin Marine rotations, one year for North West Cape, and three years for Pine Gap.193,198–200 At North West Cape, Australia would pay the US the remaining value of what it built, and keep running the station: it's our only transmitter for talking to submarines underwater.201,202 Expect the US to share less intelligence. Deciding what's done from Australian soil is worth it.

M113 armoured vehicles

LATE FEES

0.1% of a milestone's value for each week it's late. The fees applied to almost 3,100 separate items of work.216

It was a $388 million upgrade contract. In 2006 Defence told the minister its late fees were worth “about $1 million per year”, the smallest of the pressures on the contractor it listed. Queensland Health payroll

THE SETTLEMENT

The State drops its claims against the contractor and promises not to sue. The contractor fixes 35 priority defects.217

The new payroll had failed, and it had 94 significant defects when the deal was negotiated. The rest became the State's problem. Victoria, 2014

East West Link

CLAUSE 58

If a court strikes down the road's planning approval, the State pays the consortium's delay costs: up to $2.5 million a day, plus some fees and finance costs.219

Victoria's Auditor-General found the consortium asked for this clause, and for a letter from the Treasurer promising to honour the contract. Both broke with standard practice.
